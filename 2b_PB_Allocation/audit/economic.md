# Economic allocation from raw EXIOBASE

## What does Hartmann's Total GVA imply?

### Takeaway

The sector factor 0.000197293218763215 follows the paper. The published workbook's factor is 2.109 times larger. An independent calculation from the original-size (7,987 x 7,987) MRIO distinguishes them to machine precision.

### Cited findings

- Let i and j index industry–region pairs, r index regions, x denote gross output, V direct GVA, v_i=V_i/x_i, Z_ij=A_ij x_j and L=(I−A)⁻¹. For target j, g_j = ∑_i v_i L_ij. The type-I multiplier m_j=g_j/v_j, hence total GVA H_j=m_j V_j=x_j g_j when v_j is nonzero. For first-tier origin α_rj=(∑_{i in r} Z_ij+1_{r=home(j)} V_j)/(∑_i Z_ij+V_j). Sector share F_j=H_j∑_r (P_r/P_W)α_rj/G_r, G_r=∑_{i in r}V_i. These are bookkeeping identities conditional on the six satellite components, regionalisation and weights chosen. Disjoint all-sector total value shares do not follow. Hartmann explicitly warns against addition across sectors or regions with overlapping chains. [Hartmann et al., Eqs 1–4 and 6–10, pp. 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-07797-w)
- Independent script uses A.txt, x.txt, Y.txt, factor_inputs/F.txt from local EXIOBASE 3.9.6 ixi 2022, asserts matching axis labels, solves a linear system for L's target row, column and column sums. No FYR functions or precomputed economic vectors enter the reconstruction. Baseline published population weights were read as literals from the upstream code. ### Inferences

Network share is Vnet/x_j; multiplying by F_j analytically cancels x_j, but not GVA intensity, sector supply locations, or the choice of product valuation. Neither a different sector size alone nor a putative fraction that is pharma changes the result if one holds the allocation intensity constant. The original denominator uses six components (taxes on production, wages, capital consumption, net surplus), excluding product taxes, land rents and resource royalties. A test with all nine factor inputs gives g=0.999999999989 for this target. Avoid generalising H_j=x_j to arbitrary incomplete sets of value-added categories or all sectors.

### Gaps

Direct GVA zeros are a special case where direct division to obtain the multiplier is undefined. The reduced H_j=x_jg_j can be used if positive output and a defined embodied value are available. This does not affect GB Chemicals nec. Sector-to-network turnover data are outside the MRIO.

## Which Total FCE equation is best supported?

### Takeaway

With the branch price, physical output and saved footprint, 29.41687% is the best-supported central climate utilisation under Hartmann’s reference to Oosterhoff. All three reported FCE variants count direct final expenditure twice in the own-sector link.

### Cited findings

- Hartmann Eq. 5 delegates the downstream scaling to Oosterhoff. Hartmann p.4 explicitly describes a sector selling only to end consumers as having total equal to its own direct FCE. Its upstream suppliers receive some of that FCE. Purely multiplicative Eq. 5 notation cannot describe zero direct FCE multiplied by a finite factor to obtain a positive number. Oosterhoff actually constructs additive channel contributions, which avoids this singular case. [Hartmann pp. 3–4](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-07797-w)
- Write FR_kr as the share of region r's sum of household, NPISH and government final consumption for globally produced industry k. Let c_k=∑_i L_ik, f_i=(c_i−L_ii)/L_ii. Oosterhoff Eq. 3 assigns diagonal entries of S to c_i. Eq. 4 explicitly applies to **off-diagonal entries** and applies the i-indexed supplier vector f as f eᵀ. Thus the normalised allocation from final product k to supplier i is K_ik=L_ik(1+f_i)/c_k for i≠k, K_ii=1. Because L_ii(1+f_i)=c_i, the same expression already gives exactly 1 on the diagonal without an added identity. Final FCE factor F_i=∑_r p_r∑_k K_ik FR_kr. Regional FR sums are one; K represents overlapping attributed supply chains and its column sums are not constrained to one. Oosterhoff Eq.7 explicitly uses final product k's (their j's) consumption, not supplier i's. Their Fig. 2 grants 100% of direct consumer value to the B2C producer and additional shares to its purely B2B suppliers. [Oosterhoff et al. Eqs 2–9 and Fig. 2, pp. 7–9](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/311056680/1_s2.0_S0959652623007321_main.pdf)
- PBAESA forms S_roof + L + L_f on ALL entries (rows 552, 555), giving normalised own-industry diagonal 2. It then weights each row by the supplying sector's own FR as (n,1) (lines 570–574). FYR's corr_FR restores buyer-specific FR but adds a standalone FR_j to an expression that already includes it, so F_corr_FR−F_best=0.00016002906085006, exactly the direct FCE factor. corr_FR_f also switches to buyer's f instead of supplier's f, contrary to the outer product f eᵀ. A one-industry model with A=0, FR=1 gives 2 for all three variants and 1 for the derived rule. This is a local own-demand error, separate from intentional supply-chain overlap across sectors. [Upstream lines 531–576](https://github.com/RWTH-LTT/pbaesa/blob/d9329215521feba86005caac65bfa7d0404efb5a/pbaesa/allocation.py#L531-L576) [FYR variants](https://github.com/ALIFEPLAYS/FYR_code/blob/035538e33cd0223a5805ece2632e86b03e35eac4/2b_PB_Allocation/tools/audit_from_raw.py#L142-L156)

### Inferences

The 15.0–23.2% interval is a collection of implementation outputs, not a defensible methodological uncertainty interval and misses the strongest reading. With upstream population weights corrected to sum to one, corrected utilisation is 29.41949%. FCE credits a supplier for downstream consumption enabled by its product. Sector-average final demand, buyer mix and trade geography are especially weak proxies for therapeutic end use of a bulk mAb. Oosterhoff used known customer countries, a company-turnover share and an alternative capital treatment: its case endogenised capital assets in 2011 EXIOBASE; Hartmann used its standard 2022 ixi matrix. Therefore 29.42% is an equation-based reconstruction on Hartmann inputs, not a replay of the earlier case study. [Oosterhoff sections 2.3 and 3.3](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/311056680/1_s2.0_S0959652623007321_main.pdf)

### Gaps

No author confirmation or original Oosterhoff worked spreadsheet was obtained. A literal unrestricted reading of Eq. 4 in isolation duplicates the diagonal, but conflicts with its adjacent prose, Fig. 2, and Hartmann’s own worked explanation. Distinguish this sourced interpretation from a published correction.

## Is the sector/network/downscaling boundary defensible?

### Takeaway

The classification is verified. Ex-works bulk-substance output is the right *kind* of denominator, but cost is only a proxy and the actual sector technologies differ starkly. Use a fixed hypothetical scenario for optimisation experiments and avoid claims about an independently validated entitlement.

### Cited findings

- Public concordance gives NACE 21.1 and 21.2 solely p24.d; p24.d goes to i24.d. The same i24.d also includes charcoal, fuel additives and multiple biofuels, and p24.d collects many other chemical classes. Good classification does not identify biologics-specific sourcing. Raw sector GVA has zero wages across all three skill groups and zero remaining net operating surplus; 16,225.50 out of €16,233.04m is consumption of fixed capital, with €7.54m in production taxes. In contrast the modeled plant has material labour cost. This questions representativeness especially for geography or alternative income allocation, whether or not it alters the aggregate output identity. [Primary NACE concordance](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EXIOBASE/00-concordances-public/main/concordances/international/nace2_exiobase3p.csv) [Primary ixi mapping](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/EXIOBASE/00-concordances-public/main/concordances/exiobase/exiobase3p_exiobase3i.csv) [EXIOBASE](https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.15689391)
- Oosterhoff recommends sector-relative turnover and Hartmann recommends consistent system economic value, processes, temporal and geographic scopes. Starting from 861 × 4.65 kg/batch, 4,003.65 kg/year, multiply 1,000 g/kg × $92.09802/g, divide by 1.2766 USD/GBP, multiply 90.6/81.1 then divide by 0.85276 GBP/EUR and by 10⁶ to obtain €378.3834m/year (approximately, based on model cost), 0.84566% of sector output. Verify SuperPro 2019 vs EXIOBASE current 2022 million EUR and annual physical quantity. Same multiplier and numerator recover 64.8987242% primary. [Hartmann usage notes](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41597-026-07797-w) [Oosterhoff Eqs 10–11](https://backend.orbit.dtu.dk/ws/files/311056680/1_s2.0_S0959652623007321_main.pdf) [branch price and quantities](https://github.com/ALIFEPLAYS/FYR_code/blob/035538e33cd0223a5805ece2632e86b03e35eac4/2b_PB_Allocation/data/allocation_inputs_20261119.json)

### Inferences

Sector budget and turnover proxy cancellation is not proof of representativeness. Cost is neither GVA nor a measured price, and is not a hard floor for a transfer price, loss-making enterprise, contract arrangement or price under cross-subsidy. Technology and capacity utilisation differ across configurations. Purchasing cost and sales at basic prices are distinct valuations. A meaningful price range would use comparable bulk drug-substance transfer/contract manufacturer prices or full bounded fees and work through accounting perimeter. Oosterhoff's upstream/full-chain assignment is a scope rationale for a cradle-to-gate footprint, though FCE's consumption origin is downstream: no separate supplier allocation should be added alongside embodied supplier burdens. Use the total-GVA sector factor once at network level; avoid addition of full-chain budgets for individual plants/suppliers. The branch numerator is explicitly incomplete.

### Gaps

No independently measured bulk mAb contract value or verified plant-level procurement geographies were available. The raw data demonstrate sector membership and arithmetic, not representativeness of those proxies. The companion script and saved stdout reproduce the numbers above; it reads only the economic sources and supplied saved footprint, with optional repeat of the branch ATP cross-check.
